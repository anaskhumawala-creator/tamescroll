! tamescroll scriptlet rules (CC0) — engine-injected +js(...) filters that
! call our own clean-room scriptlets. Kept separate from the cosmetic
! rule files (youtube.txt etc.): these compile INTO the adblock engine,
! not the settings-pane surface layer.

! YouTube ad-free stream, no watch-click stall. Sets one field on the
! outbound /player request so InnerTube grants a stream with no ad and no
! SABR backoff — the backoff our response-side strip cannot undo (the
! multi-second freeze owner reports #9/#12).
! Provenance (clean-room): public protobuf reverse-engineering of Google's
! own API (https://iter.ca/post/yt-adblock/), NOT uBO/AdGuard code.
! test: on a youtube.com /watch SPA click, the outbound
!   POST /youtubei/v1/player body carries
!   playbackContext.contentPlaybackContext.isInlinePlaybackNoAd:true and no
!   pre-roll plays; video still plays (player red line).
! [unverified] — needs owner phone; emulator is never served real ads.
www.youtube.com,m.youtube.com##+js(trusted-set-request-field, youtubei/v1/player, playbackContext.contentPlaybackContext.isInlinePlaybackNoAd, true)

! Pre-roll video ads on a HARD navigation, and the fake buffering that
! comes with them.
!
! MEASURED 2026-08-25 (spikes/forceplayer.py, spikes/ttfp.py):
! Blocking ad REQUESTS kills the tracking and serving calls but cannot
! touch a pre-roll -- its stream is googlevideo.com, the same origin as
! the real content. Pruning the ad fields out of the embedded response
! DOES kill the ad, but it does NOT kill the penalty attached to it:
! InnerTube ships a SABR backoff instruction with the stream worth ~80%
! of the ad's duration (YouTube's "fake buffering"; see
! https://iter.ca/post/yt-adblock/). So pruning alone bought a black
! screen for as long as the ad would have run -- 24-37s to first frame,
! which is owner reports #9/#12 "loads a lot".
!
! The fix is to stop editing the embedded response and instead force the
! player onto the CLIENT-SIDE /youtubei/v1/player request, which the
! isInlinePlaybackNoAd rule above already reshapes into a stream that has
! no ad and therefore no backoff. Dropping `streamingData` is what does
! that: with no stream embedded, the player must ask, and asking is the
! path we control. Measured: exactly ONE extra /player fetch, first frame
! 8.3s/9.0s (from 24-37s), no ad, no .ytp-error. An SPA click -- which has
! always taken this path -- is 6.8s, so this brings a hard nav essentially
! in line with it.
!
! The ad fields are still pruned alongside. They are no longer what
! suppresses the ad (the reshaped request is), but leaving ad slots in an
! object the page can still read is a needless second chance to render
! one.
!
! TRADE, stated because it touches the player red line: the page now has
! no embedded fallback stream, so if that one /player request fails the
! player shows YouTube's own error instead of playing. The SPA path has
! always had exactly this dependency and works, which is the evidence
! this is survivable -- but it is the reason to keep videoDetails and
! everything else intact rather than pruning the object wholesale.
!
! test: on a cold load of a www.youtube.com/watch page,
!   window.ytInitialPlayerResponse.streamingData is undefined, exactly one
!   POST to /youtubei/v1/player is made, no .ytp-ad-skip-button appears,
!   and the content video reaches currentTime>0.3 within ~10s.
! DESKTOP ONLY for now. Everything above was measured in WebView2 on
! www.youtube.com. m.youtube.com keeps the old field list until the same
! numbers exist from the owner's phone: dropping the embedded stream is a
! player-red-line change, and shipping it to a device blind is exactly the
! trade VISION.md refuses (a broken player is worse than an ad).
www.youtube.com##+js(trusted-prune-window-json, ytInitialPlayerResponse, streamingData, adSlots, adPlacements, playerAds, adBreakHeartbeatParams)
m.youtube.com##+js(trusted-prune-window-json, ytInitialPlayerResponse, adSlots, adPlacements, playerAds, adBreakHeartbeatParams)
