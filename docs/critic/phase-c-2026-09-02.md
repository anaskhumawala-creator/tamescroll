## C1 — WRONG-NUMBER. The "no trade" is the coast window, not the clock

**CLAIM AUDITED.** §13a: *"Every column improves as the clock speeds up … There
is no exposure/phantom trade along this dial at all — the only cost is GPU duty.
That makes `VERDICT_MAX_INTERVAL_MS` the cleanest lever in the system."*

**THE CONFOUND.** The fix of `5b26a04` makes the arm call
`setVerdictCadence(dt * stride)`, and `person-track.setVerdictCadence` derives
`blurredCoastMs` from that number. So each row of the §13a table changes **two**
things at once — how often a verdict lands, *and* how long a track coasts
without one:

| row | verdicts | cadence told | blurredCoast |
|---|---|---|---|
| k=4 | 540 | 2000ms | 4000ms |
| k=3 | 720 | 1500ms | 3000ms |
| k=2 | 1080 | 1000ms | 2000ms |
| k=1 | 2160 | 500ms | **1250ms** |

"More verdicts" and "shorter coast" are different claims and the second is the
interesting one, because a longer coast is exactly what produces phantom.

**FALSIFIER, RUN.** A gitignored one-line copy of `arch-arms.mjs`
(`diff --strip-trailing-cr` shows exactly one changed line, 304) lets the cadence
handed to `person-track` be pinned while the verdict frames are thinned as
before, plus the arm option set every other bench uses (see C2):

```
$ cd app/gaze && VDT=1 node bench/_critic-cadsep-full.mjs        # the diagonal, as published
arm          verdicts  told   coast   EXPOSURE  FALSECOVER   PHANTOM  births
k=4 (2.0s)        540   2000    4000      30.0s      169.5s    533.0s     160
k=3 (1.5s)        720   1500    3000      25.5s      139.0s    465.0s     174
k=2 (1.0s)       1080   1000    2000      17.5s      110.5s    361.0s     223
k=1 (0.5s)       2160    500    1250      11.5s      102.5s    343.5s     309

$ VDT=1 FIXCAD=1500 node bench/_critic-cadsep-full.mjs           # coast held at the k=3 control
arm          verdicts  told   coast   EXPOSURE  FALSECOVER   PHANTOM  births
k=4 (2.0s)        540   1500    3000      32.0s      153.0s    383.0s     186
k=3 (1.5s)        720   1500    3000      25.5s      139.0s    465.0s     174
k=2 (1.0s)       1080   1500    3000      13.0s      122.0s    499.0s     198
k=1 (0.5s)       2160   1500    3000       9.0s      141.0s    784.0s     239
```

**RESULT.** With the coast held constant, speeding the clock from k=3 to k=1:

| column | published (diagonal) | coast held |
|---|---|---|
| EXPOSURE | 25.5 → 11.5 (−14.0s) | 25.5 → **9.0** (−16.5s) |
| FALSE COVER | 139.0 → 102.5 (−36.5s) | 139.0 → **141.0 (+2.0s)** |
| PHANTOM | 465.0 → 343.5 (−121.5s) | 465.0 → **784.0 (+319.0s, +69%)** |

**Only exposure genuinely responds to the clock.** The entire improvement in the
other two columns is the coast shortening, and with the coast fixed the phantom
column moves the *other* way by 69%. Reproduced on the bare arm too (482.5 →
822.0, +70%) and at every pinned coast: at 2000ms, k=4 → k=1 is 551.5 → 1020.0.

**AND §13a CONTRADICTS §10n IN THE SAME DOCUMENT.** §10n, measured
independently: *"**Verdict COUNT is what drives phantom**, not demotion: +28.0s
and +31.5s for 59 extra verdicts scattered at random — an 8% increase in verdicts
costing 15-19% more phantom. Phantom is his loudest complaint, so this is the
constraint on every cadence lever."* The fixed-coast experiment agrees with §10n
exactly; the §13a diagonal is what disagrees, and it does so because it moved a
second variable.

**WHAT SURVIVES.** The diagonal is a *defensible model of the dial* — on a device
`effZoom` really does drive both, so lowering `VERDICT_MAX_INTERVAL_MS` really
does shorten the coast alongside. What does not survive is the stated
**mechanism** ("as the clock speeds up"), the generalisation ("no trade along
this dial at all"), and the derived instruction that the only cost is GPU duty.
The cost is phantom-per-verdict, and it is paid back by a shorter coast — a
cancellation, not an absence, and one that stops cancelling the moment anything
else moves the coast.

**AND HALF THE ADVERTISED SPAN IS UNREACHABLE.** `tuning.mjs:118` clamps
`VERDICT_MAX_INTERVAL_MS` to **[1200, 4000]**, so the k=2 (told 1000) and k=1
(told 500) rows that carry the headline cannot be reached by the constant the
section is about. At the clamp floor the bare arm reads (`FIXCAD=1200`):
k=3 **37.5s / 172.0s / 322.0s**, k=2 **16.5s / 159.0s / 383.0s** — phantom rising
with the verdict count again.

**COST IF TRUE.** The section's conclusion is an instruction to push a live OTA
constant on the grounds that it has no downside. It has one, it is phantom, and
phantom is the complaint he has repeated most.

**FALSIFIER TO CLOSE.** Publish the fixed-coast column beside the diagonal in
§13a, or state why the two variables should be reported as one.

## C3 — WRONG-NUMBER. §13b's placement benefit is the coast the median-gap inference handed it

**CLAIM AUDITED.** §13b: *"T is bisected until the arm spends the uniform budget,
so the comparison is placement against placement and never budget against
budget … At a max gap of 2.0s, spending the SAME verdicts where the picture moved
buys **8.5s of exposure and 16.0s of phantom for 31.5s of false cover**."*

**THE BUDGET IS HELD; THE COAST IS NOT.** The stride inference is the **median**
gap between verdict frames. In a starvation-dominated policy most gaps *are*
`MAXGAP`, so the median is the worst gap, not the typical one — and it varies
per window:

```
$ node bench/_critic-placestride.mjs
budget 720
PLACED gap 4  T=13.2  strides={"1":1,"2":2,"3":2,"4":13}
             told/coast per window: 2000/4000 1000/2000 1000/2000 500/1250 2000/4000 ...
             verdicts 720  frames 2160  MEAN gap 3.00 frames
PLACED gap 6  T=7.1  strides={"1":3,"2":4,"3":1,"5":1,"6":9}
             told/coast per window: 3000/6000 1000/2000 500/1250 500/1250 3000/6000 ...
             verdicts 720  frames 2160  MEAN gap 3.00 frames
UNIFORM k=3   strides={"3":18} -> told 1500 coast 3000
```

Both placed arms have the **identical mean gap of 3.00 frames** as the control,
and both hand `person-track` a completely different coast: 13 of 18 windows get
4000ms at max gap 4 (against the control's 3000ms), and 9 of 18 get **6000ms** at
max gap 6. C1 has just established that the coast is what moves two of the three
columns.

**FALSIFIER, RUN.** Same arm, same budget, same placement — coast pinned to the
control's 3000ms in every arm (shipped option set, per C2):

```
$ FIXCAD=1500 node bench/_critic-place-full-fix.mjs 4,6
arm                    verdicts  T     EXPOSURE  FALSECOVER   PHANTOM  births
UNIFORM k=3 (today)        720  --       27.5s      137.0s    460.5s     174
PLACED, max gap 4          720   13.2     27.5s      167.5s    462.5s     200
PLACED, max gap 6          720    7.1     36.0s      177.5s    452.5s     195
```

against the same file with the coast free (i.e. as §13b measured it):

```
UNIFORM k=3 (today)        720  --       27.5s      137.0s    460.5s     174
PLACED, max gap 4          720   13.2     18.0s      166.0s    438.0s     194
PLACED, max gap 6          720    7.1     50.0s      186.5s    398.0s     264
```

**RESULT.** At max gap 4, holding the coast equal:

| column | §13b published | coast held equal |
|---|---|---|
| EXPOSURE | buys **8.5s** | buys **0.0s** |
| PHANTOM | buys **16.0s** | **loses 2.0s** |
| FALSE COVER | loses 31.5s | loses 30.5s |

**Both advertised gains disappear and one inverts.** What the placed arm bought
was a longer coast in 13 of 18 windows, obtained for free because the inference
reads the median gap of an irregular policy as its cadence. The one column that
survives is the cost.

The max-gap-6 arm's collapse is likewise part coast: free-coast 50.0s, pinned
36.0s. The section's conclusion — *"the max gap is the load-bearing half of the
policy, not the threshold"* — is measured on an arm where the max gap is also
setting the coast, so it cannot separate those either.

**WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SETTLE.** It does not show event-driven placement
is worthless; it shows §13b has not yet priced it. The section's own caution
(*"NOT PROPOSED … the instrument had just been wrong three times in one night
about exactly this kind of thing"*) turns out to have been the right instinct for
a reason it did not name: the instrument was wrong a fourth time, in the arm
built to check it, in the way its own comment half-predicted. That comment guards
against an irregular policy being handed a stride of **1**; it does not notice
that *any* stride an irregular policy produces disagrees with the control's.

**FALSIFIER TO CLOSE.** Re-run `cadence-place.mjs` with the cadence handed to
`person-track` held equal across all arms, and re-derive §13b's paragraph.

## C4 — WRONG-NUMBER. The app tells the tracker `effZoom`, not the gap it achieves

**CLAIM AUDITED.** §13: *"THE CADENCE THE TRACKER IS TOLD MUST BE THE CADENCE IT
GETS"*, and the table row *"1500 (correct) | 3000ms | 1500ms | yes"*.

**THE APP DOES NOT DO THAT.** `init-entry.js:4036` calls
`setVerdictCadence(effZoom)`, and `effZoom` (line 3392) is
`min(VERDICT_MAX_INTERVAL_MS, max(ZOOM_INTERVAL_MS, lastVerdictMs * 4))` — the
*scheduled* interval, capped. `cadence.mjs`'s own measured table says his Redmi
is **cap-limited in both arms**: `effZoom = min(2000, 5000) = 2000` and
`min(2000, 2912) = 2000`. And §10n, in the same document, records that his
**achieved** gap in the same 90s window is **1.55s**, because a cut drags the
next verdict forward.

So the repo already holds both numbers and knows they differ. The fix picked the
achieved gap for the thing the app feeds the cap into. §13 replaced one mismatch
(told 500, gets 1500) with another (told 1500, app tells 2000).

**FALSIFIER, RUN.** k=3 verdict frames — his achieved gap — with the cadence his
device actually hands `person-track`:

## C5 — DEAD-CHECK. One bench in the tree still runs the defect, and the stated invariant is what says it cannot

**CLAIM AUDITED.** `arch-arms.mjs:286-291`: *"A caller-supplied option is a thing
30 bench files can forget; `thin` marks every frame it silenced by moving the
reads to `_labelFaces`, so the stride is a property of the window the arm was
handed and cannot disagree with it."*

**THERE IS NO `thin`.** Every bench inlines its own thinning; 30 of them use
`{ ...fr, faces: [], _labelFaces: fr.faces }`. `bench/matrix.mjs:76-83` has two
modes and only one of them does:

```
    if (mode === 'coast') return { ...fr, faces: [], _labelFaces: fr.faces };
    return { ...fr, faces: fr.faces.map((f) => ({ ...f, _noRead: true })) };
```

A `_noRead` face is a **position-only** observation in `obsOf` — no verdict is
remade — so those frames are non-verdict frames that the inference cannot see.

**FALSIFIER, RUN.**

**RESULT.** `matrix.mjs`'s `[3, 'position']` arm — one of the three regimes that

**AND NOTHING CAN CATCH IT.** `git show --stat 5b26a04` touches no test.

**FALSIFIER TO CLOSE.** Detect a non-verdict frame by what `obsOf` will do with

## C8 — NIT. An inert knob and a bank with no freshness assert

**`MINGAP` cannot bind.** §13b describes the policy as three parts and names
`MINGAP` *"the app's own `ZOOM_INTERVAL_MS` floor (400ms, ~1 banked frame)"*. In
`placedSet`, `i` always exceeds `last`, so `i - last >= 1` is a tautology:

```
$ node bench/_critic-mingap.mjs
MAXGAP 4 T 13.2:  MINGAP=1 -> 721   MINGAP=0 -> 721   MINGAP=-99 -> 721
MAXGAP 6 T 7.1:   MINGAP=1 -> 720   MINGAP=0 -> 720   MINGAP=-99 -> 720
```

**`bank/deltas.json` has no staleness guard.** `cadence-place.mjs` throws if the
file is absent or missing a tag, but never checks its `__meta`
(`{"GATE_SIZE":16,"rate":10}`) against `scene-gate.GATE_SIZE`. A4 was filed for
exactly that on `cuts.json`, and `arch-arms.assertCutsFresh` is the fix; the new
bank shipped without the equivalent.
