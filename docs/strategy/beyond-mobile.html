Tame Scroll beyond the phone

For ZVBased on the code at release 1120 (v0.1.120)Checked against the goals in VISION, plan and the original handoff

The goal, in your words: an app even grandparents can use, that removes the doom scroll and the algorithms pushing filthy content. Get the phone app fully right first: Android, then iPhone. Desktop waits. When it comes, it is probably an extension or a short guide to tools that already work, not a second app.

  • On the phone, Tame Scroll becomes a calm browser. The address bar is visible by default, the platforms open already cleaned, and Focus mode hides the address bar for people who only want their platforms. Ads are blocked by default, with a switch to show them.
  • "Done correctly" means it opens instantly, video blur behaves, any link works, it can be your default browser, sign-in and autofill work, and onboarding takes under a minute. Then it gets proper Play Store and App Store releases.
  • Checking against your goals turned up four gaps: the plain-language playbook, the step that pairs the app with Screen Time or Digital Wellbeing, protection against switching things off at 1am, and translations. They are in the table below.

What Tame Scroll is on the phone

The website line still fits: the same platforms, none of the machinery. As a browser it gains one clause: it goes anywhere, and the platforms come cleaned.

Privacy browsers

Brave, DuckDuckGo

Block trackers and ads. The feed, Shorts and recommendations are untouched.

Clean-YouTube apps

ProTube, CleanTube

YouTube only, mostly paid, no gaze protection.

Screen-time apps

Digital Wellbeing, one sec

Limit, lock or pause. They don't change what the feed shows.

Tame Scroll

a calm browser

Five platforms open cleaned, with optional blur. Any other site opens normally. It never locks and never nags.

What "a proper app, done correctly" means

Grouped by what a person notices. Items already being worked on in other threads are marked.

Feels instant instant launch thread

Opening the app shows your platforms straight away, with no loading screen.

Blur behaves video blur thread

Thumbnail blur stays. Turning video blur off sticks for that video (PR #18). The phone default is your call there.

A real browser

A start page with big platform tiles and one "search or type a site" box above them. While browsing, the address sits at the bottom where a thumb reaches, with big Back and Home buttons. No tabs in 1.0: they add a concept a grandparent doesn't need. Today a link is accepted only when it belongs to the tile's platform (link_host_belongs). That becomes "any link opens, platforms get cleaned".

Default browser

The Android manifest already registers the app for http and https links. Becoming the default browser mostly means accepting the links it currently turns away.

Ads as a choice

Blocked by default. "Show ads" turns off network blocking, pre-roll scriptlets and the ads rules, which already sit apart from the cleaning rules in the code.

Safe to type any address

Android's WebView has Google Safe Browsing built in, so a phishing warning comes almost free. Check that the app doesn't turn it off.

Sign-in that just works

Password manager autofill and passkeys, tested on your phone before release.

Onboarding under a minute

Gender choice, blur level, Focus mode as an offer, the home icon (already in 1118), and the optional lock-pairing step from the playbook.

Keeping the 1am promise

Your original plan said there should be no instant way to switch protection off inside the app. A browser with a Focus mode switch, an ads switch and a blur switch moves away from that. Here is a way to keep the switches without making them a 1am escape hatch:

  • Tightening is instant, loosening waits. Turning Focus mode on or blocking ads happens at once. Turning them off asks you to wait ten seconds first. This is the one sec idea you liked: "pause feels chosen, block feels punished".
  • Lock pairing stays optional. The playbook shows how to put the app behind Screen Time or Digital Wellbeing, with someone you trust holding the code. No custom locking code in the app, as planned.

This trades a little friction for protection, so it's your decision. It's the first item below.

The mobile ladder

Each step rests on the one before it.

  1. Finish what you noticed in progress

    Instant launch, the video blur fix in PR #18, and the phone default for video blur. These are the things you feel every day.

  2. Android becomes a browser

    The start page with one search box above the tiles, any link, default browser, search engine choice, Focus mode, the ads switch, and the switching-off pause if you want it.

  3. Play Store release

    The listing pack in your hands (upload key, copy, three screenshots, privacy page), the legal page the legal thread is writing, an autofill check, and the first version of the playbook on the website.

  4. iPhone

    Needs a Mac to build and an Apple Developer account. Every iOS browser uses Apple's WebKit, which Tauri uses there anyway. Safari-style content blockers can't stop YouTube's ads because they come from youtube.com itself, so the scriptlet approach you already use is the one that works. It ships as our own app, not a Safari extension, which would be too fiddly for this audience. The Core ML parity check that is waiting on a Mac happens here too.

  5. Earn the other platforms

    Measured passes for Reddit and X, and the rules watcher, so a platform update doesn't quietly break a rule.

  6. Reach more people

    Translations of the app and the playbook, starting with Urdu and Arabic. Tablets get a wide layout from the same app.

Desktop, later

Desktop waits until the phone is done. When it comes, there are three ways to do it, and you don't need to pick now. A desktop app already exists in the repo (the same Tauri code, Windows build, with working ad blocking), so nothing is lost by waiting.

Now, almost free

Point to tools that work

A "Tame Scroll on your computer" page on the website: uBlock Origin for ads, Unhook for YouTube's feed and Shorts, HaramBlur for blur. Your own plan already suggested pointing at HaramBlur on desktop. No gender filter, but it costs an afternoon.

Later, likely best

Our own extension

Your original plan had this. The rules and the blur engine already run as code injected into the page, which is how an extension works. One build covers Chrome, Edge, Brave and Firefox. Chrome's newer extension rules limit network blocking, so ads may need the scriptlet route there.

Possible, more work

The desktop app

It exists and works on Windows. It still needs a one-window design, auto-updates and a signed installer, and Mac support needs its own ad blocking. Keep it parked.

An extension reverses your earlier "browser extension stays dead" ruling in CLAUDE.md. It's your call, and it can wait until the phone ships.

Decisions for you

Settled: the address bar is visible on first open, and Focus mode stays off until the person turns it on, because store reviewers judge the default.

  1. Switching-off pause.Should turning off Focus mode, ad blocking or blur wait ten seconds, or happen instantly?
  2. Ads switch scope.One global switch, or global plus per platform. Per platform lets people support creators on YouTube only.
  3. Blur outside the five platforms.Thumbnail blur on every site, or only on the platforms.
  4. Default search engine.A privacy-respecting default such as DuckDuckGo, or the person picks during onboarding.
  5. Desktop path, when the phone ships.The guide page now, then an extension, or revive the desktop app.

Becoming a browser reverses lines in docs/VISION.md, docs/plan.md and CONTEXT.md ("no general-purpose browsing", ads "never user-toggleable"). They should be updated once the legal thread settles the wording, so later sessions don't argue against your decision.
